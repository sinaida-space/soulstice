# The style workbook

Source: Sinaida's own worksheet *Как найти свой стиль и голос* (sin.ai.da, 2026), a
six-section instrument covering the same ground as Soulstice but built for solo
written work rather than a guided session. Everything below is drawn from it. It adds
two things the layers did not carry: a few card shapes, and a set of between-session
exercises. The card shapes fold into the layers named. The exercises are the first
homework this skill hands out, and they belong to Journal mode and to the note that
follows a Compass, never to a passage in progress.

Its own framing, used throughout and worth keeping: **style is not found, it is
recognised.** It already exists in how she looks at a screen, what enrages her in
other people's work, what catches her breath. The instrument removes noise so she can
hear it. Say a version of this at the close if it fits.

---

## Card shapes to fold in

### Layer 5 — the opposition scale

She places her own work on three axes out of a set of poles, then defends the
placement.

> **Q:** Three of these pairs. Where does your work actually sit on each, not where
> you would like it to?
> **header:** Axes
> multiSelect: true

- Silence ↔ noise
- Order ↔ chaos
- Cold ↔ warm
- Slowing down ↔ speeding up
- Shown ↔ withheld

Beat two, on the pair she was least sure about: "You hovered on that one. Which end
is the work now, and which end is the work you keep meaning to make?" The gap between
current and intended placement is the finding, not the placement itself.

The workbook's instruction on this card: discriminate by the relation to the viewer,
not by genre. A card that sorts by medium or subject has been built wrong. Rebuild
the poles from her own vocabulary once you have it.

### Layer 5 — the director card

> **Q:** Your body of work is a film director. Who, and why that one?
> **header:** Director

Four named directors chosen to be near-misses for what she has already described: one
for control, one for atmosphere, one for coldness, one for excess. She corrects in
"Other", and the correction is a sentence about how the work treats whoever is
watching. Keep it verbatim for the Compass.

### Layer 11, or Compass time — the three-sentence manifesto

Three open-text completions, asked one card at a time, no options:

1. "I make work for people who…"
2. "My work is always about…"
3. "My work will never be about…"

The third sentence is load-bearing and connects to the reverse constraint below. Put
all three in the Compass verbatim, under **Three directions** or just above it.

---

## The reverse constraint

From the workbook's third section. Naming what she will never do is not a smaller
move than naming what she does; it narrows the field faster. A limit is the contour
of the form rather than a cage around it. Every "never" she states is a wall of the
shape she is already working inside.

Use it two ways: as the third manifesto sentence above, and in Layer 1 as a
follow-up after the inversion card:

> **Q:** One thing you will not put in the work, regardless of the commission or the
> trend. Which?
> **header:** Never

Four options built from her medium. Beat two asks what that refusal protects. This is
the same instrument as Layer 9's refusal card, aimed at craft rather than at the
present moment. Run only one of the two in a single passage.

## Between-session exercises

The workbook's starred tasks. Hand out at most one at a time, only in Journal mode or
in the note after a Compass, never during a passage. Each is a retrieval device that
works on the body's timescale rather than the session's. Record what she brings back
as a dated Journal entry.

**Museum without a guide.** Open Pinterest, Behance or Are.na with no brief. Save
everything that catches, no justification, at least forty images in one sitting. Lay
them out together and write the three words for what is common to them. Repeat once a
quarter. The drift of the collection across quarters is a record of how her eye is
changing, and that record is Journal material.

**Body resonance scale.** Take ten works, hers or other people's. For each, write a
number from one to ten for the physical response, not the intellectual one. The
high-resonance works are the reference points. Pairs with Layer 8: the somatic marker
that layer isolates is the same signal this exercise measures across a set.

**Genealogy map.** Draw it. Top: the current work. Below: five direct influences.
Below that: where those came from. Look for what repeats down the generations, and
for where she is the first in her line. Pairs with Layer 2 and Layer 4.

**Letter to the critic.** Write a letter to the inner critic. Not a defence, an
explanation: what she is making and why. Show it to nobody; the writing is the whole
exercise. Pairs with Layer 1, and it is a safe thing to hand someone who left a Fear
layer with the belief named but not yet tested.

**Three objects.** Pick three physical objects from the room that could stand for the
work. Do not explain the choice while making it. Then read what they say about the
aesthetic. Pairs with Layer 5 and gives fast material for a thin Field layer.

**One manifesto-project.** Make one piece that breaks one of the "never" rules. Do
not publish it. Notice what the breaking felt like. This is the workbook's last
exercise and it belongs after a Compass, as a test of whether a stated "never" is a
real boundary or an inherited one.

## The synthesis line

The workbook closes on a formula worth borrowing for the Compass when all three arcs
have produced material:

> Style is the intersection of what you see (taste), what you do without deciding to
> (technique), and what frightens you to say out loud (voice). Where the three meet
> is the language.

Map it onto the arcs: taste is Arc II layer 5, technique is Arc III layer 8, voice is
Arc I layers 1 and 6. If a Compass has strong material in all three, name the
intersection under **Three directions**. If one of the three is thin, that is the
finding, and it goes under **Still unresolved**.
