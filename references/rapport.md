# Getting someone to actually talk

Craft notes on delivery, for every layer, not only the voice-finding ones. A
well-built card asked in the wrong register still fails: it reads as a form. This
file is about the difference between an instrument and an interview room.

Read `references/cards.md` first for the shape of a card. This file is about the
voice around the shape.

---

## What this is not

The techniques below are adapted from interviewing traditions that exist to get a
person to disclose more than she otherwise would. Several of those traditions —
counterintelligence elicitation training in particular — include tools built for
exactly that and nothing else: flattery deployed as leverage, a false statement
planted to provoke a correction, feigned ignorance to invite a person to over-explain.
Those tools assume the subject does not know she is being worked and would not
consent if she did. **None of that belongs here, and none of it is used.** The person
on the other side of this instrument is not a target; she is the one it exists to
serve, and everything she is being drawn out to say is for her own use. Deception has
no function in a room where the subject and the beneficiary are the same person —
it would not produce a truer answer, only a manipulated one, and a manipulated
answer is worthless to a Compass that has to survive contact with her own life
afterward. What is borrowed below is the rapport-building and memory-retrieval half
of these traditions, which does not depend on deception at all and is validated on
its own terms.

---

## Three sources, three specific moves

**PEACE, the rapport-based interviewing model that replaced coercive interrogation
in England and Wales.** Its second phase, Engage and Explain, establishes that
information gathered under a relaxed, explained, low-pressure frame is more accurate
and more complete than information extracted under pressure — not just kinder,
better data. The operational form here: say what a layer is doing and why before
running its first card, in one plain sentence, no jargon. Never open cold. A person
who understands the shape of what is being asked, and why, gives fuller answers
than one being tested.

**The cognitive interview (Fisher & Geiselman), built for eyewitness recall and
directly reusable for autobiographical recall of any kind.** Memory has more than
one retrieval route, and a question that only tries one route gets a thin answer.
Four mnemonics, all directly usable as card mechanics:

- *Context reinstatement.* Before asking what she remembers, ask her to place herself
  back in the room: where she was, what time of day, who else was there, what was
  playing or not playing. A card can do this as its stem rather than as a separate
  step: "The last time this happened — where were you, and what was in the room?"
- *Report everything.* Resist the instinct to ask for the one right answer.
  A multiSelect card that lets several true things stand at once will produce more
  than a single-select card demanding the one that matters — the pruning can happen
  in beat two.
- *Change order.* Ask about the middle before the beginning, or the end before the
  middle. A recurring pattern hides in whichever order feels unnatural to describe,
  because that order has not been rehearsed into a story yet.
- *Change perspective.* "What would someone standing next to you have noticed?"
  produces material a first-person question does not, because it routes around
  her own account of herself.

**Tactical empathy (Chris Voss, adapted from FBI hostage negotiation, built for
non-adversarial use in negotiation, coaching, and therapy contexts since).** Three
moves, all about how a follow-up is phrased rather than what it asks:

- *Mirroring.* Repeat her own last few words back, verbatim, as the opening of the
  next card's stem, before asking anything new. It costs nothing and it is the
  single fastest way to make a follow-up feel like it was heard rather than
  generated. "You said 'a hobby I called a career.' ..."
- *Labeling.* Name the feeling you are inferring, tentatively, before asking about
  it: "That sounds like it cost something to say" rather than "how did that feel."
  A label offered as a guess invites correction, and correction is where the real
  material comes from — this is the same mechanism the two-beat rhythm already
  runs on, applied to tone instead of content.
- *Calibrated questions.* Open with "what" and "how," never "why." "Why did you make
  that" reads as a request to justify and produces a defence. "What was happening
  when you made that" reads as curiosity and produces an account. Every card stem
  in this skill should pass this test before it ships.

## Register

The instrument is allowed to sound like a person who is actually curious, not like
a form with feelings simulated on top. Concretely:

- Contractions, always. "What's" not "what is." A card stem that reads like a legal
  document has already lost.
- Short sentences before the question, not after. One line of framing, then the ask.
- No therapeutic throat-clearing. Never "I'd love to understand more about..." or
  "it sounds like you might be feeling..." as a preamble — say the plain version.
- The description field can carry warmth the label cannot. "A technical failure in
  front of witnesses who know the tools better" is doing more work, and sounding more
  like a person, than a clinical label would.
- Silence is a valid beat. Not every card needs a beat two immediately; sometimes the
  next card is a plain "go on," per the two-beat rhythm's own guidance in
  `cards.md`, and sometimes it is nothing — let an answer sit for a moment before the
  next card fires, in whatever the delivery surface allows.
- Never perform enthusiasm about a painful admission. "That's such a powerful answer"
  after she names something costly is the tell of a form pretending to be a friend.
  Move to the next card instead. Respect reads as taking the answer seriously enough
  to act on it, not as praising it.

## Where this changes existing layers

Every beat-two in this skill can be rebuilt with a mirror before the question:
quote her exact words, then ask. This is not a new mechanism, it is a tightening of
one already in use — `cards.md`'s two-beat rhythm already depends on quoting her
material back as options; mirroring extends that into the question stem itself,
not only the answer set.

The resonance cluster in layer 4 (`layers.md`, `voice.md`) is the layer most built
around cognitive-interview mechanics: context reinstatement in its opening card,
report-everything via multiSelect, and a change-of-perspective follow-up. Run it
warm or it reads as trivia rather than evidence — see the failure mode in `voice.md`.
