# Finding the voice

Method notes for the layers that do the actual work of locating a voice: layer 4
(Voices), layer 5 (Field), layer 6 (Standing), layer 7 (Poisons). Read this before
running new cards in any of them. `references/rapport.md` covers the delivery —
how to ask so she actually answers. This file covers what to ask.

The problem these four layers exist to solve: most of what an artist can say about
her own work on request is either borrowed vocabulary (what critics say, what
statements say) or defensive vocabulary (what protects her from a feared judgment).
Neither is the voice. The voice is closer to what she cannot help returning to, what
moved her before she had a theory about why, and what she would still make if nobody
were going to see it. None of that arrives from being asked "what is your artistic
voice." It arrives sideways, through specific memory and specific material.

---

## Five methods, and what each is for

**Career Construction Interview (Savickas).** Vocational psychology's finding is
that an admired figure named early is rarely admired for the whole person — she is
carrying one specific thing the admirer's self-concept did not yet have: a skill,
a permission, a form of confidence, a way of being difficult and still accepted.
The interview's role-model question is designed to externalise that gap so it can
be looked at directly instead of lived out as endless comparison.

*Use it for:* layer 4. The gap between "who I admired" and "who I am" is where a
borrowed want is still doing quiet work.

**Means-end chain / laddering (Gutman; Reynolds & Gutman).** A technique from
consumer research, originally clinical before that: an attribute a person keeps
choosing is never the actual reason. Ask "why does that matter to you" and climb —
attribute, to consequence, to personal value — usually two to three rungs. The
technique already lives in this skill as the downward arrow (`unpacking.md`), which
climbs toward a feared belief. Laddering climbs the same way toward a value instead
of a fear, and layer 5 currently has no card that does this: it finds what recurs
but never asks what the recurrence is *for*.

*Use it for:* layer 5. Take a named recurring element and ladder up, not down.

**Narrative identity and self-defining memories (McAdams).** People hold a small set
of memories that function as anchors for who they are becoming, and these differ
from ordinary memories in that they are vivid, emotionally intense, and revisited
often. McAdams also found that the *shape* of how someone narrates a setback —
turning bad into good (a redemption sequence) versus good curdling into bad (a
contamination sequence) — predicts how she carries her own history forward.

*Use it for:* layer 5, to find the memory where something first felt authored rather
than assigned. The shape of how she tells it (redemption or contamination) is itself
a finding, not incidental to it.

**Art therapy and the presented self versus the working self.** A recurring finding
in art therapy on professional identity is that the version of a maker that appears
in a statement, a bio, a curated feed is a self-portrait she chose, and it is
routinely a different person from the one in the room at the moment of making —
more mechanical, more anxious, less resolved, less finished. Locating the gap between
the two, without collapsing one into the other, is itself diagnostic.

*Use it for:* layer 6. Standing already asks what she wants concluded about her.
This adds the other half: who is actually there when nobody is concluding anything.

**Aesthetic emotion and self-referential engagement.** Frisson — the involuntary
chill, the skin response — is a validated marker of peak aesthetic response, distinct
from liking or from judged quality, and it correlates with the personality trait
openness rather than with expertise or taste. Separately, neuroimaging of aesthetic
judgment finds that the works a person rates as most personally moving activate the
same regions implicated in self-referential thought, autobiographical memory and
mind-wandering — a physiological argument that "this moved me" and "this technically
succeeded" are not the same measurement. A body of work made mostly to be seen being
made is measuring something different from a body of work made because it changed
something in the maker.

*Use it for:* layer 7. Comparison poisons judgment by substituting "did this land
with someone" for "did this actually do something to me." A card that asks her to
sort her own list by which measurement was actually running is a diagnostic for the
poison, not a lecture about it.

## Cards this produces

**Layer 4 — the role-model gap.** Who represented a thing you didn't have yet, and
is the gap still open. See `layers.md`, layer 4, "the role-model gap." Savickas.

**Layer 4 — resonance, cross-domain.** What has actually produced a chill, a lump in
the throat, gooseflesh — regardless of medium, regardless of whether it was art at
all. A kettle, an accent, opera heard outdoors in the wrong season. This is new
territory for the layer: everything else in Voices is subtractive, sorting impulses
into hers and arrived. Resonance is additive — it goes looking for what already,
demonstrably, reliably moves her, on the theory that a voice is built out of what a
person is helplessly responsive to, not only out of what she has decided to reject.
Frisson research, cognitive interview retrieval mechanics (see `rapport.md`).

**Layer 5 — laddering up.** Take one recurring element from her own list and climb:
what does that give you, and what does *that* give you. Two rungs, sometimes three.
Means-end chain.

**Layer 5 — first yours.** Of everything on the list, when did something first feel
authored rather than assigned, and what happened right after she noticed. McAdams,
self-defining memories.

**Layer 6 — the working self.** Who is in the room at two in the morning that the
statement does not mention, and which of the two the work would be better for. Art
therapy, the presented/private self.

**Layer 7 — two motives.** From her own list: one piece made mostly to find something
out, one made mostly to be seen finding it out. Which she trusts now as a measure of
her own judgment. Aesthetic emotion, self-referential engagement.

## Failure modes specific to voice-finding

**The curated answer.** Ask "what moves you" without a memory to anchor it and she
will describe her own taste as she would present it in a bio. The anchor — a specific
kettle, a specific summer, a specific person's voice — is what keeps the answer from
becoming wall text. If an answer could go in an artist statement unedited, it is not
yet a finding.

**Confusing admiration with identification.** "I love this artist's work" and "I have
been trying to become this artist" are different facts and the role-model card exists
to separate them. If she defends the admired figure instead of examining the gap, the
card was answered as a taste question rather than an identity question. Ask again with
the beat two, which is not optional here.

**Treating resonance as trivia.** A card about kettles and opera can read as whimsy if
delivered flatly. It is not decoration; it is the same evidentiary weight as everything
else in the instrument, run through a different retrieval route. See `rapport.md` on
register — this cluster fails first if it is asked in the wrong tone.
