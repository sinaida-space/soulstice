# Method toolkit

Techniques borrowed from coaching, clinical interviewing and therapy-adjacent practice,
stripped of their clinical apparatus. Use them as moves inside the layers, not as
frameworks to announce. Never name the technique to the user.

---

## Clean Language (David Grove)

The core discipline of this whole instrument. Ask about the user's material using only
the user's own words, adding nothing.

The workhorse questions, applied to any noun or phrase they just used:

- "And what kind of X is that X?"
- "And is there anything else about X?"
- "And where is X?" / "And whereabouts?"
- "And that's X like what?" (invites metaphor)
- "And what happens just before X?"
- "And then what happens?"
- "And what would X like to have happen?"

**Why it matters here:** the moment you paraphrase someone's word into your own, they
begin answering about your word. An artist who says a piece came out "murky" and gets
asked about "ambiguity" has just been moved into a curatorial register and lost the
thing. This matters most with words that do not translate cleanly, which is exactly
where the charge usually sits.

**Use when:** they say a word with charge on it, a strange word, or a metaphor.
**Do not:** stack three clean questions in a row. It becomes hypnotic and evasive.

**In card form:** the clean move becomes an option set built out of her own word.
She called a piece "murky". The next card asks what kind of murky, and all four
options use her word to name four different kinds of it. Never build an option set out
of your paraphrase of what she said.

## Immunity to Change (Kegan & Lahey)

Four columns. Use in layer 1 (Fear) and any time a stated goal has been stated before
and not acted on.

1. **Commitment.** What do you want that you are not getting?
2. **Doing / not doing.** What are you actually doing that works against it? Behaviours,
   not attitudes. Be specific and non-judgemental.
3. **Competing commitment.** "If you did the opposite of column 2, right now, fully,
   what is the worry?" The worry reveals a second, hidden commitment, usually protective:
   *I am also committed to not being seen as pretentious.*
4. **Big assumption.** The belief that makes the hidden commitment sensible. *If I claim
   scale, I will be exposed as a fraud.*

Then: design one small, safe, real-world test of the assumption. Not a leap. A test.

**Why it matters here:** it reframes stuckness as intelligence rather than weakness.
Both feet are on pedals for a reason. Artists respond to this far better than to
willpower framings.

## Motivational interviewing: change talk

Do not argue with resistance; ask the person to argue with themselves.

- Ask for both sides: "What is good about keeping it this way?" before "and what is the
  cost?"
- Ask for elaboration on any sentence containing want, need, could, should, or reason.
  Those are change talk. Everything else can pass.
- Use scaling: "On 0 to 10, how much does this matter?" then "why not a lower number?"
  Never "why not higher", which produces defence.

**Use when:** a decision is being circled without commitment.

## Values work (ACT)

Values are directions, not goals. A goal can be completed; a direction can only be
walked. "Be shown at Ars Electronica" is a goal. "Make work that changes how a room
feels to be in" is a direction.

Moves:
- Sort every stated ambition into goal or direction. Ask which goals serve which
  direction. Orphaned goals are usually layer 4 material.
- **The eulogy move,** used only in layer 11 and only once: "someone who knows the work
  well describes your practice in thirty years. What do you want them to be able to say
  truthfully?" Powerful and easily overused; one deployment per passage.
- **Willingness:** "what discomfort are you willing to carry to go that way?" A value
  with no cost attached is decoration.

## Internal Family Systems, light touch

Useful vocabulary, dangerous depth. Stay at the level of "part of you".

- "Part of you wants the large public piece. Part of you keeps making small private
  things. What does each one want?"
- "What is the small-private part afraid would happen if it stopped?"

**Hard stop:** do not do parts work on trauma, do not name protectors and exiles, do not
run any dialogue past two turns. If it starts going somewhere clinical, stop and say so.

---

## Externalising (narrative practice)

Move the problem out of the person. "The block" rather than "you are blocked".

- "When did the doubt first show up in your practice? What was happening then?"
- "What does it feed on? When is it weakest?"
- "Name a time it was there and you worked anyway. What was different?"

**Unique outcomes** are the goal: the exceptions to the story they tell about themselves.
One counter-example does more than an hour of analysis.

---

## Solution-focused moves

Use sparingly; they are quick and can feel glib in this context.

- **Exception question:** "when was it not like that?"
- **Scaling:** as above.
- Skip the miracle question. In an artistic-identity context it produces fantasy rather
  than material.

## The downward arrow

Lives in `references/unpacking.md` with its own worked ladder, because it is the
method for layer 1 and the answer to every label that arrives in "Other". In short:
"suppose that happened, what would it mean?", four times, one card per rung. Never
"why does that frighten you", which asks for a theory and gets one.

---

## Concreteness ladder

The single most reliable technique in this instrument. Any abstract answer gets pushed
down one rung:

    concept → category → episode → sensory detail

"I'm afraid of judgement" → "whose judgement" → "when did that last happen" → "what
were they actually wearing, what did they say, what did you do with your hands".

Insight lives at the bottom two rungs. If a session feels intelligent but nothing is
shifting, you are stuck at the top of the ladder.

The concreteness ladder and the downward arrow move in opposite directions and are
both needed. Concreteness goes outward, toward the episode: who, when, wearing what.
The arrow goes inward, toward the belief: and what would that mean about you. Run
concreteness first to get a real event, then the arrow on that event.

## Reflection and summary

- **Simple reflection:** repeat their words back. Invites continuation.
- **Amplified reflection:** overstate slightly. "So the market is entirely irrelevant to
  you." They will correct you toward truth. Use rarely; it is a small provocation.
- **Double-sided reflection:** hold both halves of a contradiction in one sentence
  without resolving it. This is the main tool at arc breaks.

---

## Constraint prompts

For layer 5 and layer 8, when the material is thin:

- "Make the same work with a tenth of the budget. What survives?"
- "One material, one gesture, no screen. What is it?"
- "Your five favourite works of yours are destroyed and cannot be remade. What do you
  do on Monday?"
- "You may only make one more piece. What is it?"

## The correction beat

Silence has no equivalent in a card interface, so this skill does not try to fake one.
What silence buys in a room is the second answer, and the second answer is where the
truth is. Cards buy the same thing by a different route: give her something specific
to disagree with, then ask what is wrong with it.

Every pick is followed by a card that attacks the pick. See the two-beat rhythm in
`references/cards.md`. This is the load-bearing mechanism of the whole skill, and it
is the reason a card session can go deeper than an open-question session rather than
shallower: people correct a wrong sentence far more precisely than they generate a
right one from nothing.

Corollary: **your options are allowed to be wrong on purpose.** In layer 5 and layer 9
they should be. Four near-misses produce a better answer than one open question.

---

## What not to use

- **Ikigai diagrams.** The four-circle version is a Western mistranslation and produces
  career advice, not artistic material.
- **Personality typologies** of any kind. They give the user a category to hide in.
- **Positive affirmation and reframing.** Closes inquiry.
- **"What's really going on here is…"** Interpretation ends the user's own work.
- **Goal-setting frameworks** (SMART, OKR). That is for-tee-too's job, not this one.
